# Two algebraic sections in a complete three-cell family

This successor to the regular single-section family retains two distinct roots
of one polynomial, the three original GCAD cells, and four integration charts.
It is a sufficient construction for separated regular sections on a fixed
native cube. Moving brackets, collisions, general towers and a global resolved
real atlas remain separate acceptance gates.

Admission consumes the existing verified native decomposition and two proposed
brackets, which it checks over the complete admitted domain. It does not infer
these brackets from a sample or claim a general automatic CLI factory.

For sections `a(x) < b(x)`, write `d=(b-a)/2`. The four maps are
`a*(1-t)`, `a+d*t`, `b-d*t` and `b+(1-b)*t`. Their positive integration measures
are respectively `a`, `d`, `d` and `1-b`. Reversed interval directions carry no
extra negative measure. The middle cell has two descendants; it is retained
once in the source-cell inventory.

Native exact polynomial division proves the second divided-difference identity
modulo both defining root equations. The construction certifies its positive
unit, the root gap and all interval widths over the closed admitted domain.
Certified root selectors and brackets identify the branches; identical
polynomial coefficients cannot merge their numerical callbacks. The original
integration variable stays separate from the two formal root variables.

The complete density uses the existing causal-cell phase, symbolic subtraction,
Laurent expansion, native function maps and evaluator codecs. Root functions
remain exact implicit functions in the symbolic graph. Numerators are composed
with the maps, and Symbolica supplies their required chain-rule derivatives.
There is no formula in radicals, alternative root solver, numerical-dual
endpoint reduction or evaluation-order-dependent branch choice.

bounded identity search. This is an optional sufficient proof, not a general
saturation algorithm or a limit on the defining polynomial's degree. Every
inverted factor must have the original chart's unit authority. An open-cube

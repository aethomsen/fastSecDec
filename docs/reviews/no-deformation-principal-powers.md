# Exact principal-power presentations

This auxiliary resolver operation recognizes a single native polynomial
generator of the form `p = u*g^k`, with `u` a proved unit in the original
localization and mark `d = k*e`. It retains the actual source and history and
issues an explicit presentation of `(p,d)` by `(g,e)`. It does not replace a
physical denominator or discard its multiplicity.

## Mathematical boundary

The relation preserves normalized orders and marked cosupport. Under an
admissible controlled transform, division by the exceptional power preserves
the same power identity. The corresponding projection and exceptional test
transforms also preserve the relation. This is strong marked equivalence,
using the test transformations and product identities in
[Bierstone–Milman 2008, Definition 2.5 and Lemma 3.7](https://ems.press/content/serial-article-files/41043).
It is not equality of ideals and cannot be stored in a same-ideal normalization
receipt.

For the actual native coefficient source `(y^10,6)` arising from the cusp
`x^3+y^5`, the operation proves the smaller auxiliary presentation `(y^5,3)`.
The recursive-center driver does not yet consume that receipt. It must retain
an explicit presentation edge, readmit the selected center against the original
source, and transport the relation through the original physical history.
This proof-only milestone does not resolve that cusp or complete the driver.

There is no generatorwise radical operation. A mixed ideal such as `(x^2,y^2)`
does not equal `(x,y)^2`: the latter also contains `x*y`. Mixed-generator inputs
therefore remain unchanged. Differentiating an equivalent presentation does
not assert equality of its derivative ideals.
